# Predicting Retrospective Intraday Trend Labels from One-Minute Market Features: Separate and Shared Multi-Scale Models

**Working manuscript draft — 26 October 2026**

**Data status:** internal BovDB data; not cleared for public release.

**Working target journal:** *Journal of Forecasting* (Wiley; provisional, not submitted).
**Running title:** Intraday Label Forecasting
**Submission status:** internal draft; author details and data-owner clearance are outstanding.

## Abstract

We study whether completed one-minute OHLCV and activity features can reproduce retrospective intraday trend labels generated by the internal B0 procedure at 5-, 10-, and 15-minute scales. The analysis uses WIN and IND futures from January through June 2026. We compare separate binary multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) with a shared encoder and three masked-loss heads, alongside logistic regression, histogram gradient boosting, causal momentum, majority-label, and soft-score baselines. Dates are split chronologically into training, validation, and a final 25-session test period. On the test set, the paired difference in Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC), independent minus shared MLP, is 0.0052 for 5 minutes (95% session-block bootstrap interval −0.0002 to 0.0102), 0.0033 for 10 minutes (−0.0057 to 0.0121), and approximately 0.0000 for 15 minutes (−0.0052 to 0.0051). The intervals include zero at every scale. Histogram gradient boosting attains the highest pooled MCC among the evaluated models at 10 and 15 minutes; the independent MLP is slightly highest at 5 minutes. A soft-score MLP reduces mean absolute error by 31.1% relative to the training-mean constant when reproducing the deterministic B0 vote fraction. These findings concern prediction of retrospective labels only. They do not validate B0 against independent market truth and do not establish a trading edge.

**Keywords:** intraday data; futures; multi-task learning; time-series validation; market labels; label agreement

## 1. Introduction

Intraday market studies often compare forecasts against labels constructed from price movement or a segmentation procedure. When a label is assigned retrospectively using subsequent observations, a model can be evaluated on whether information available at a timestamp predicts the label later attached to that timestamp. That task is distinct from observing a contemporaneous market state and from forecasting executable returns.

This study examines three B0 label scales, 5, 10, and 15 minutes, aligned to one-minute candles. Its main comparison is between three independently trained binary classifiers and one shared representation with three output heads, following the general multi-task learning setup of sharing representations across related tasks (Caruana, 1997). We also compare simple and nonlinear baselines, a hard majority label, and a continuous vote-fraction target. The design uses a held-out chronological test period and treats trading sessions, rather than individual candles, as the unit for uncertainty estimation.

The scope is deliberately narrow. The available local data support WIN and IND futures for the selected period. WDO, WSP, and ISP are absent from the local candle-and-label snapshot; lower-activity or sparse roots are excluded from the confirmatory sample. The study asks whether the input features reproduce B0 labels, not whether those labels are true market states.

## 2. Data and label construction

### 2.1 Sources and population

The analysis uses the internal BovDB market database `bovdb-v3.1-internal.2.sqlite` and B4 label database `bovdb-labels-B4-v1.sqlite`. The audited source hashes are recorded in `reports/final_test_metrics.json`. The label database contains retrospective B0 targets for 5-, 10-, and 15-minute horizons and a derived `trend_mean` record. The latter is used as the hard majority label and as a deterministic soft target (`mean_score`) when all component labels are available.

WIN and IND are the confirmatory roots selected from the observed local snapshot using median daily traded quantity, number of trades, and session coverage. Their contracts are retained as separate ticker series. There is no continuous-contract stitching or adjustment. All contracts and roots observed on a given date share the same temporal split. Five ticker contracts appear in the final test predictions: INDM26, INDQ26, WINM26, WINQ26, and WINV26. Contract-level metrics are descriptive and are supplied separately.

The data cover 119 observed sessions between 2 January and 30 June 2026. The common date split assigns 71 sessions to training (2 January–15 April), 23 to validation (16 April–22 May), and 25 to final testing (26 May–30 June). The final test contains 20,488 rows with complete features. Target-specific test counts range from 20,395 to 20,444; 20,332 observations have all three target labels and form the paired primary comparison sample.

### 2.2 Target semantics and alignment

Each target is aligned on the same `(ticker, date, time)` key as its one-minute candle; no row or date shift is applied. The decision-time features are considered available after the timestamped one-minute interval closes. B0 labels are retrospective and may depend on later candles within that session. Thus, all reported models estimate the label attached ex post to the same timestamp. They do not forecast next-session movement, provide independent validation of the label, or demonstrate that the label was observable in real time.

Missing targets are not imputed. Independent classifiers use the available training rows for their own head. The shared model uses per-head masks. The paired independent-versus-shared comparison uses only rows with all three labels available. The majority and soft-score models use the common-label intersection.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Causal one-minute features

We compute 11 inputs from the completed candle and past observed candles: log returns over 1, 5, and 15 consecutive observed one-minute rows; candle body and range divided by close; population standard deviations of one-minute log returns over 5, 15, and 30 rows; and `log1p` of volume, trade count, and traded quantity. Windows reset at gaps, ticker changes, and session boundaries. There are no synthetic minutes, forward fills, or feature imputations. Incomplete warm-up rows remain in the cache but are excluded from model fitting and scoring. B0 labels, SIDEWAYS fields, segment endpoints, and fields derived from future candles are excluded from the inputs.

### 3.2 Temporal design and preprocessing

Dates are ordered chronologically and assigned wholly to a single split, reflecting the temporal order required for evaluating time-series predictors (Bergmeir & Benítez, 2012). The final models are fit using training dates; validation metrics are retained for development reporting and the final test is scored once under the frozen candidate set. Four expanding validation folds are used for development, each with ten successive validation sessions and no final-test dates. Standardization parameters are fit on the applicable training rows only. Training rows are not shuffled.

The frozen neural-network settings are seed 20261026, batch size 256, five epochs, two dense hidden layers of 32 and 16 ReLU units, dropout 0.1, and Adam with learning rate 0.001. Binary heads use sigmoid outputs and binary cross-entropy; the soft-score model uses a sigmoid output and mean squared error. The shared model has three masked binary heads. No hyperparameter search was conducted after the candidate set was frozen.

### 3.3 Models and metrics

The evaluated candidates are: a most-frequent training-class classifier; a causal 5-minute momentum sign rule; logistic regression (`C=1`); histogram gradient boosting (learning rate 0.1, 50 iterations, at most 15 leaves, L2 regularization 1, no early stopping); independent MLPs for the three scales; a shared three-head MLP; an MLP for the hard majority label; and a soft-score MLP compared with the training-mean constant.

For classification, we report MCC, balanced accuracy, macro F1, class prevalence, confusion counts, and (for probability-producing models) Brier score and log loss. MCC incorporates all four confusion-matrix cells (Chicco & Jurman, 2020). The Brier score is a probability scoring rule (Brier, 1950). Regression to `mean_score` is evaluated with MAE and MSE. Metrics are pooled over rows for descriptive model performance and repeated by root, fixed temporal block, and ticker contract. The three temporal blocks contain 8, 8, and 9 sessions. Candle rows are not treated as independent replicates for the primary inferential contrast.

### 3.4 Paired inference

The primary comparison is independent MLP versus shared MLP on the same rows with all three targets observed. On development folds, paired session-level MCC differences are summarized with a circular moving-block bootstrap of five sessions and 10,000 replicates, a fixed-length block resampling method for dependent observations (Künsch, 1989; Politis & Romano, 1992). Two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests across the three targets receive Holm correction (Holm, 1979). These are development-only results.

On the final test, we report the pooled paired MCC difference and a circular five-session moving-block bootstrap interval using 10,000 replicates. We report no final-test p-values. Probability metrics describe reproduction of retrospective B0 labels and are not presented as calibrated probabilities of an independently observed market state.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Development results

Across the 40 out-of-fold development validation sessions, mean paired session-level MCC differences (independent minus shared) were 0.0002 for 5 minutes (95% block-bootstrap interval −0.0071 to 0.0069), −0.0005 for 10 minutes (−0.0038 to 0.0029), and −0.0031 for 15 minutes (−0.0096 to 0.0031). Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon p-values were 1.00 for all three comparisons. The development analysis therefore did not indicate an advantage for shared supervision.

### 4.2 Final test results

The paired primary test results are summarized below. The exact sample for each comparison contains 20,332 rows with all three labels available.

| Target | Independent MCC | Shared MCC | Difference | 95% session-block interval |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5-minute | 0.4934 | 0.4882 | 0.0052 | [−0.0002, 0.0102] |
| 10-minute | 0.4436 | 0.4403 | 0.0033 | [−0.0057, 0.0121] |
| 15-minute | 0.4306 | 0.4306 | −0.0000 | [−0.0052, 0.0051] |

Each interval includes zero. The independent and shared MLPs show no clear separation on the held-out sessions. For the 5-minute target, the independent MLP has pooled MCC 0.4925, compared with 0.4896 for histogram gradient boosting and 0.4891 for logistic regression. For 10 minutes, histogram gradient boosting is highest among these models (MCC 0.4513); for 15 minutes it is also highest (0.4448), while the independent and shared MLPs are both approximately 0.429. Results are similar between WIN and IND at the pooled root level; the complete root and block tables are in `reports/final_test_metrics.csv`.

The soft-score MLP obtains MAE 0.2837 and MSE 0.1210, compared with MAE 0.4119 and MSE 0.1918 for the training-mean constant. This is improvement at reproducing `mean_score`, a deterministic fraction of B0 votes. It does not establish probability calibration or label validity.

### 4.3 Temporal and contract breakdowns

The final test is reported in three pre-fixed blocks and separately for WIN and IND. The 15-minute MCC increases in the last block for the independent MLP, shared MLP, and histogram gradient boosting; this pattern is descriptive and does not establish a regime effect. Per-ticker contract metrics are available in `reports/final_test_contract_metrics.csv`; they are retained as sensitivity summaries and are not used for inferential claims or model selection.

## 5. Discussion

The principal finding is that, under the frozen feature set and small MLP budget, joint three-scale supervision did not clearly outperform separate models. This agrees with the development analysis and persists on the final held-out dates. The strongest pooled model varies by target scale: the independent MLP is marginally highest at 5 minutes, while histogram gradient boosting performs better at 10 and 15 minutes. The substantial reduction in soft-score error over a constant baseline suggests that the features contain information about the B0 vote fraction, but this target is derived from the same retrospective labels and is not independent ground truth.

The evaluation supports conclusions about reproducing B0 labels for the local WIN/IND sample. It does not support claims about the true direction of the market, the uncertainty of market states, or profitable trading decisions. The absence of a clear independent-versus-shared difference also gives no empirical basis for the optional representation-learning extensions in the present project scope.

## 6. Limitations

First, B0 labels are retrospective and not independently validated. Their dependence on subsequent session candles means the target is not directly observable at the feature timestamp. Second, the data are an internal snapshot with limited scope: WIN and IND only, 119 sessions, January–June 2026. WDO, WSP, and ISP have no candles and labels in this snapshot; lower-activity roots are outside the confirmatory analysis. Third, B1/B2/Fuzzy labels do not provide adequate matched coverage for an independent-label comparison. Fourth, the contract-level breakdowns contain few tickers and are descriptive. Fifth, no transaction costs, order execution, slippage, returns, or trading strategy are evaluated. Finally, test uncertainty is estimated over only 25 sessions and cannot establish broad market generalization.

## 7. Data and code availability

The source SQLite databases and derived row-level predictions are internal and are not included in this repository. The repository retains contracts, frozen evaluation configuration, scripts, manifests, and compact reports. Reproduction requires authorized access to the same source snapshot and environment. Source and label hashes, software versions, seed, run duration, test dates, and the one-pass evaluation output are recorded in `reports/final_test_metrics.json`. The pooled and subgroup summaries are regenerated from saved test predictions by `scripts/summarize_final_test.py`; this script does not refit a model.
