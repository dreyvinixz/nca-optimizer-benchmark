\title[Optimizer Benchmark for Neural Intraday Trend Classification]{%
  A Benchmark Study of Evolutionary and Swarm Intelligence Optimizers
  for Neural Intraday Trend Classification in Brazilian Futures Markets}

% NOTE: For double-blind submission, do NOT include \author blocks here.
% Author information goes in the separate titlepage.tex file.

\abstract{%
Financial asset price forecasting in high-frequency intraday environments represents one of the most challenging applications of computational intelligence, characterized by severe non-stationarity, regime shifts, and low signal-to-noise ratios. Although Multilayer Perceptrons (MLPs) are widely employed for trend classification, their generalization depends critically on hyperparameter tuning across non-convex search spaces. However, existing financial modeling studies overwhelmingly evaluate single, default optimizers without controlled budget constraints, often suffering from temporal data leakage. This paper presents a controlled benchmark evaluating five distinct optimizer families—Random Search (RS), Genetic Algorithm (GA), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Differential Evolution (DE), and Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO)—for tuning an MLP classifier on 5-minute B3 Mini-Index futures data. All five algorithms are evaluated under an identical computational budget of $N_{\text{eval}} = 1,500$ evaluations per seed, operating within a leakage-free 60/20/20 chronological temporal split and a compound MCC-driven objective ($0.60 \times \text{MCC} + 0.40 \times F_1$). Experimental results demonstrate that GA and DE achieve superior out-of-sample classification balance ($\text{MCC} = 0.231 \pm 0.012$ and $0.228 \pm 0.014$), significantly outperforming Random Search ($p < 0.01$, Cohen's $d > 1.2$). In out-of-sample financial backtesting with transaction costs, the GA-tuned MLP yields a Net Return of 18.4\% and a Sharpe ratio of 1.42. These findings highlight the necessity of equal-budget benchmarking and leakage-free temporal validation for deploying robust evolutionary neural models in quantitative trading.
}
