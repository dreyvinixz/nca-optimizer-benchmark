\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

% --- Move 1: Territory (Domain motivation, market specifics, challenges) ---
Short-term price forecasting in high-frequency derivatives markets represents one of the most computationally demanding applications of neural computing \cite{bashir2026evobagnet, jesus2025tda}. Among traded financial instruments, futures contracts are particularly sought after by institutional and retail market participants due to their high liquidity, leverage potential, and utility for both risk hedging against price fluctuations and speculative trading strategies \cite{souza2025iccsa, al_bannoud2026accelerating}. These contracts establish an agreement to purchase or sell an underlying asset at a predetermined price on a future date, rendering them exceptionally sensitive to order flow dynamics, short-term market expectations, and macroeconomic announcements \cite{jarrow1981forward, cardoso2022bovdb}. In Brazil, the Mini-Index futures contract (symbol: WIN) on Brasil Bolsa Balc\~{a}o (B3), referenced to the Ibovespa stock index, exhibits immense daily trading volume and constitutes a prime domain for computational intelligence research in financial time series \cite{souza2024dataset, bashir2026evobagnet}. However, price series derived from 5-minute intraday candles exhibit severe non-stationarity, low signal-to-noise ratios, structural regime shifts, and rapid dissipation of arbitrage patterns as market participants exploit temporary inefficiencies \cite{makridakis2018statistical, dhingra2025solar}. These characteristics render accurate directional trend classification a methodologically challenging task that demands both robust feature engineering and principled hyperparameter optimization \cite{billah2024nca_moving_average}.

% --- Move 2: Model & Search Space (ML models, features, feature selection) ---
Early attempts to formalize market patterns and predict directional movements relied on static linear models, such as autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) techniques \cite{box2015time}. As finance and computer science converged under the computational intelligence paradigm \cite{jiang2021applications}, data-driven machine learning (ML) models gained widespread adoption for financial classification tasks. Supervised learning algorithms---including Random Forests (RF) \cite{ballings2015evaluating}, Support Vector Machines (SVM) \cite{naik2019optimal}, Multilayer Perceptrons (MLP) \cite{ecer2020mlp_ga_pso}, and 1D Convolutional Neural Networks (1D-CNN) \cite{bandgar2025finchronos}---have demonstrated strong capabilities in learning complex, non-linear decision boundaries from historical market features. A central prerequisite for building effective classifiers involves technical analysis, wherein mathematical transformations of price and volume series---such as Simple Moving Averages (SMA), Exponential Moving Averages (EMA), and standard deviation envelopes---are computed to capture trend momentum and volatility regimes \cite{billah2024nca_moving_average, hunter1986exponentially}. Nevertheless, computing high-dimensional pools of technical indicators introduces significant redundancy and noise, creating a feature space that degrades classifier generalization and increases computational overhead \cite{singh2022feature, lei2012feature}. Consequently, applying feature selection techniques to extract compact, highly informative indicator subsets becomes vital for stabilizing training dynamics and improving out-of-sample robustness \cite{kumar2014feature, souza2025iccsa}.

% --- Move 3: Optimization Paradigm (Hyperparameter tuning, MOAs) ---
Beyond feature engineering and selection, classifier performance depends critically on hyperparameter configuration \cite{ding2011optimizing}. In neural network architectures such as MLPs and 1D-CNNs, structural parameters (e.g., hidden layer width, filter counts), regularization coefficients (L2 weight decay $\alpha$, dropout rate $p_{\text{drop}}$), and training settings (learning rate $lr$, mini-batch size $B$) jointly define a complex, non-convex optimization surface over a mixed discrete-continuous search space $\bm{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ \cite{jovanovic2024wann, dhingra2025solar}. Traditional gradient-based algorithms such as Stochastic Gradient Descent or Adam operate effectively on differentiable weight-loss surfaces but cannot directly navigate the non-differentiable, combinatorial hyperparameter landscape \cite{faris2018gwo_review, rajwar2023exhaustive_review}. To overcome these limitations, metaheuristic optimization algorithms (MOAs)---spanning evolutionary algorithms such as Genetic Algorithms (GA) \cite{goldberg1989ga} and Differential Evolution (DE) \cite{storn1997de}, as well as swarm intelligence paradigms such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) \cite{kennedy1995pso} and Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO) \cite{faris2018gwo_review}---have gained substantial prominence for automated hyperparameter tuning in neural computing applications \cite{al_bannoud2026accelerating, bashir2026evobagnet}. These population-based methods maintain diverse candidate pools and employ stochastic exploration-exploitation operators, enabling effective traversal of rugged, multi-modal fitness landscapes without requiring gradient information \cite{faris2018gwo_review}.

% --- Move 4: Literature Gap (3 major flaws) ---
Despite the growing literature on metaheuristic-tuned financial classifiers published in premier neural computing journals \cite{dhingra2025solar, al_bannoud2026accelerating, bashir2026evobagnet, bandgar2025finchronos}, existing financial machine learning studies overwhelmingly suffer from three major methodological flaws:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Budget Inequality Flaw}: Benchmark studies frequently evaluate metaheuristics under unconstrained or unequal evaluation counts (e.g., comparing a GA running for 100 generations with a PSO running for 30 iterations) \cite{dhingra2025solar}. Because stochastic search performance scales monotonically with candidate evaluation count, reported performance superiority in prior studies is often an artifact of unequal evaluation budgets rather than inherent algorithmic efficiency.
    \item \textbf{The Temporal Data Leakage Flaw}: Standard randomized $K$-fold cross-validation or randomized data shuffling is routinely applied to time series data \cite{makridakis2018statistical, dhingra2025solar}. Shuffling 5-minute intraday bars breaks chronological continuity, allowing future price dynamics to contaminate training sets and producing artificially inflated validation metrics that fail under live trading execution.
    \item \textbf{The Classification-Economic Disconnect Flaw}: Models are overwhelmingly tuned and evaluated solely on raw classification accuracy or cross-entropy loss \cite{chicco2020mcc}. However, raw accuracy treats false positive and false negative signals symmetrically and ignores class imbalance. In financial markets, uncalibrated signals incur severe transaction fees and slippage, leading to net losses despite high nominal accuracy.
\end{enumerate}

% --- Move 5: Solution, Framework & Contributions ---
To resolve these methodological limitations, this paper presents a controlled multi-optimizer benchmark framework for short-term neural intraday trend classification on 5-minute B3 Mini-Index futures data. Incorporating Information Gain feature selection to extract a compact 7-indicator subset from 66 raw technical indicators, we systematically evaluate five distinct optimizer families---Random Search (RS), Genetic Algorithm (GA), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Differential Evolution (DE), and Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO)---across four diverse classifier backbones (MLP, SVM, RF, 1D-CNN). All optimizers operate under a strictly equal candidate evaluation budget cap of $N_{\text{eval}} = 1{,}500$ evaluations per seed within a leakage-free 60/20/20 chronological split, optimizing a compound MCC-driven objective ($0.60\text{MCC} + 0.40F_1$) designed to resist class imbalance distortion. Through non-parametric Friedman and post-hoc Wilcoxon--Holm analyses, we demonstrate statistical competitive parity among GA, DE, and PSO under budget equality, and validate the top-ranked configuration via out-of-sample financial backtesting under transaction costs.
