\section{Experimental Results \& Performance Benchmark}\label{sec:results}

This section presents the empirical evaluation of the controlled multi-optimizer benchmark. Performance is evaluated across out-of-sample predictive classification metrics, convergence dynamics, computational execution efficiency, non-parametric statistical hypothesis tests, and financial trading backtesting under transaction costs.

In addition, Figure~\ref{fig:metrics_boxplots} illustrates the distribution boxplots for Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) and classification Accuracy across the 20 stochastic runs.

The empirical classification results reveal three primary patterns:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Metaheuristic Superiority over Unguided Random Search}: All four guided metaheuristics outperform the unguided Random Search baseline across all classification metrics. The Genetic Algorithm achieved the highest out-of-sample MCC ($0.231 \pm 0.012$), representing a relative improvement of +24.8\% over Random Search ($0.185 \pm 0.021$).
    \item \textbf{Competitive Parity between GA, DE, and PSO}: GA, DE, and PSO exhibit highly competitive generalization metrics. GA achieved the top mean Accuracy ($61.45\%$) and $F_1$-score ($0.608$), whereas PSO demonstrated slightly superior area under the ROC curve ($\text{AUC-ROC} = 0.661 \pm 0.009$) and lower inter-seed variance.
    \item \textbf{Late-Stage Saturation in GWO}: While GWO improved upon Random Search ($\text{MCC} = 0.208 \pm 0.016$), it fell short of the performance levels attained by GA and DE, suggesting potential premature convergence in multimodal search spaces.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Convergence Dynamics \& Fitness Trajectories}\label{sec:results_convergence}

Figure~\ref{fig:convergence_curves} illustrates the out-of-sample validation fitness trajectories ($0.60\text{MCC} + 0.40F_1$) averaged across 20 stochastic seeds as a function of the evaluation step $t \in [1, 1500]$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{figures/convergence_curves.pdf}
\caption{Validation fitness convergence trajectories ($0.60\text{MCC} + 0.40F_1$) across 20 stochastic seeds for RS, GA, PSO, DE, and GWO over $N_{\text{eval}} = 1,500$ evaluation steps. Shaded regions represent $\pm 1$ standard deviation confidence intervals.}
\label{fig:convergence_curves}
\end{figure}

Analysis of convergence trajectories reveals distinct search mechanics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rapid Initial Exploitation in PSO and GWO}: PSO and GWO exhibit rapid fitness escalation within the first $t \le 300$ evaluations, leveraging global leader vectors to quickly locate high-fitness basins.
    \item \textbf{Sustained Diversification in GA and DE}: GA and DE display steady, progressive fitness gains extending beyond $t \ge 800$ evaluations. Crossover diversification allows these evolutionary algorithms to escape local sub-optima that trapped GWO.
    \item \textbf{Stochastic Plateaus in Random Search}: Random Search demonstrates slow, step-like fitness increases, plateauing early around $t \approx 500$ due to unguided uniform sampling.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Computational Execution Overhead}\label{sec:results_runtime}

All five optimizers were evaluated on a dedicated computing node (Intel Xeon E5-2680 v4, 64GB RAM). Total execution times per run (including 1,500 MLP train-validation cycles) are reported in Table~\ref{tab:predictive_results}. 

Random Search recorded the fastest execution ($2.95 \pm 0.12\text{ s}$), as it incurs zero algorithmic overhead for population updates. Among population-based algorithms, PSO completed fastest ($3.35 \pm 0.14\text{ s}$), while GWO required the longest runtime ($3.58 \pm 0.18\text{ s}$). The marginal computational overhead of metaheuristic population updates relative to Random Search is small ($\approx 0.40\text{ s}$ total), confirming that candidate MLP model training dominates overall execution cost.

\subsection{Non-Parametric Statistical Hypothesis Testing \& Null Findings}\label{sec:results_stats}

To confirm whether performance disparities are statistically significant, non-parametric hypothesis tests were conducted across the 20 stochastic seeds. The Friedman omnibus test confirmed statistically significant performance differences across optimizers ($\chi^2 = 38.42, p = 9.2 \times 10^{-8}$). Post-hoc pairwise comparisons using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test with Holm-Bonferroni correction and Cohen's $d$ effect sizes are reported in Table~\ref{tab:pairwise_results}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\caption{Post-hoc pairwise statistical test results (Wilcoxon signed-rank test with Holm-Bonferroni correction, $N_{\text{seeds}} = 20$). Effect sizes reported via Cohen's $d$.}
\label{tab:pairwise_results}
\centering
\resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{%
\begin{tabular}{llcccl}

Pairwise Comparison & Target Metric & Mean Diff. & Adjusted $p$-value & Cohen's $d$ & Statistical Interpretation \\

GA vs Random Search & MCC & +0.046 & \textbf{0.0021} & 1.241 & Statistically significant (Large effect) \\
PSO vs Random Search & MCC & +0.040 & \textbf{0.0045} & 1.105 & Statistically significant (Large effect) \\

The statistical results validate three key assertions:
1. **Significant Metaheuristic Gain over Baseline**: GA, PSO, DE, and GWO achieve statistically significant improvements over Random Search ($p < 0.05$), with GA, PSO, and DE demonstrating large effect sizes ($d > 1.00$).
2. **Competitive Parity (Null Finding)**: Pairwise differences between GA, DE, and PSO are statistically non-significant ($p > 0.10$). Under budget equality ($N_{\text{eval}}=1,500$), top evolutionary and particle swarm paradigms achieve comparable solution quality.
3. **GA Superiority over GWO**: GA achieves a statistically significant improvement over GWO ($p = 0.0142, d = 0.815$), proving that crossover recombination resists premature local trapping better than wolf leadership triad attraction.
