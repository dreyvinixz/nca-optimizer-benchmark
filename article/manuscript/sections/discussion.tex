\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

This section interprets the empirical benchmark findings, contextualizing the search mechanics of evolutionary versus swarm intelligence optimizers, evaluating scalability, and examining local trapping risks, following the structure of Section 4 in Al Bannoud et al. \cite{al_bannoud2026accelerating}.

\subsection{Mechanistic Interpretation and Financial Trade-offs}\label{sec:disc_interpretation}

The benchmark results demonstrate distinct performance behaviors between evolutionary algorithms (GA and DE) and swarm intelligence algorithms (PSO and GWO) operating over the non-convex 6D MLP search space $\bm{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^6$.

As observed in recent surrogate benchmarks \cite{al_bannoud2026accelerating}, swarm algorithms such as PSO and GWO exhibit rapid initial convergence ($t \le 300$). This rapid exploitation is driven by global attraction operators ($\bm{g}$ in PSO and the $\bm{\alpha}, \bm{\beta}, \bm{\delta}$ triad in GWO), which pull the swarm toward the current global best candidate. While this mechanism accelerates early fitness gains, it increases susceptibility to premature local trapping when optimizing noisy neural validation loss surfaces. GWO, in particular, plateaued at a lower mean MCC ($0.208 \pm 0.016$), as its shrinking vector $a: 2 \to 0$ aggressively forces exploitation before full exploration of alternative basins.

In contrast, evolutionary computation paradigms (GA and DE) rely on stochastic recombination operators (two-point crossover in GA and vector mutation `DE/best/1/bin` in DE). These recombination operators continuously generate trial vectors that sample non-linear hyperparameter combinations. This stochastic diversification enables GA and DE to escape local sub-optima, sustaining progressive fitness improvements up to the evaluation budget cap ($N_{\text{eval}} = 1,500$). This finding confirms that evolutionary mutation-recombination mechanisms preserve population diversity across non-stationary financial landscapes \cite{bashir2026evobagnet}.

\subsection{Scalability to Complex Parameter Spaces}\label{sec:disc_scalability}

A core contribution of this work is empirical validation of the \textit{Budget Inequality Hypothesis}. In unconstrained literature surveys \cite{dhingra2025solar, rajwar2023exhaustive_review}, performance claims favoring specific metaheuristics often stem from unequal evaluation counts. When candidate evaluation counts are strictly capped at $N_{\text{eval}} = 1,500$, pairwise non-parametric tests confirm that performance differences between GA, DE, and PSO are statistically non-significant ($p > 0.10$).

This null finding carries profound methodological implications: under equal computational budgets, the performance cap of top-tier metaheuristics is primarily governed by the informativeness of the objective function ($0.60\text{MCC} + 0.40F_1$) and the intrinsic signal-to-noise ratio of the dataset, rather than minor differences in search operator design. Consequently, published claims of absolute algorithmic dominance must be re-evaluated under strict evaluation budget controls.

\subsection{Local Trapping Risks in Metaheuristic Algorithms}\label{sec:disc_trapping}

Evaluating neural classifiers strictly through out-of-sample financial backtesting highlights the critical necessity of economic evaluation. Raw accuracy improvements do not guarantee trading profitability if signals incur high trade frequency during choppy market regimes.

The GA-tuned MLP achieved an out-of-sample Net Return of $+18.4\%$ and a Sharpe ratio of $1.42$ under standard exchange fees ($0.01\%$). By optimizing for MCC rather than cross-entropy loss, the candidate search process implicitly penalized false positive buy signals during sideways market consolidations. When exchange fees were increased to high-friction levels ($0.02\%$), net profitability remained positive ($+12.0\%$), demonstrating robustness against transaction cost drag.

\subsection{Methodological Boundaries and Future Outlook}\label{sec:disc_outlook}

Several threats to validity and methodological boundaries must be acknowledged:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Construct Validity}: While the 6D MLP search space captures key structural and regularization parameters, additional neural hyperparameter dimensions (such as activation function choices, learning rate decay schedules, and optimizer momentum coefficients) were fixed to maintain a tractable benchmark.
    \item \textbf{Internal Validity}: The optimizer budget cap was fixed at $N_{\text{eval}} = 1,500$ evaluations per seed. While $N_{\text{eval}} = 1,500$ reflects realistic computational bounds for intraday retrain cycles, expanding budgets to $N_{\text{eval}} = 5,000$ could reveal asymptotic convergence behavior in high-dimensional settings.
    \item \textbf{External Validity}: Empirical evaluation focused on B3 Mini-Index futures (WIN). While WIN futures represent a highly liquid Latin American derivative asset, evaluating cross-asset generalizability across foreign exchange (Forex), commodity futures, and global stock indices remains an important avenue for future research.
\end{enumerate}
