\section{Related Work \& Theoretical Background}\label{sec:related_work}

This section synthesizes relevant literature on intraday financial forecasting, metaheuristic hyperparameter tuning, and temporal validation protocols, organized around three core scientific hypotheses.

\subsection{Financial Forecasting \& Neural Architectures}\label{sec:rel_financial}

Financial price forecasting in high-frequency intraday markets represents a benchmark application for computational intelligence \cite{billah2024nca_moving_average}. Derivatives markets exhibit extreme non-stationarity, microstructure noise, and rapid regime transitions \cite{cardoso2022bovdb}. Machine learning architectures—particularly Multilayer Perceptrons (MLPs)—have been widely adopted due to their universal function approximation capabilities \cite{ecer2020mlp_ga_pso}. However, because standard backpropagation optimizes internal weights given a fixed architecture, model generalization remains highly sensitive to hyperparameter choices.

\subsection{Metaheuristic Tuning \& The Budget Inequality Hypothesis}\label{sec:rel_budget}

A major hypothesis addressed in this study is the \textit{Budget Inequality Hypothesis}: reported performance differences among metaheuristic optimizers are frequently distorted when algorithms operate under unequal candidate solution evaluation counts. 

Metaheuristic optimization paradigms—encompassing evolutionary computation and swarm intelligence—provide principled stochastic search operators for non-convex hyperparameter landscapes \cite{goldberg1989ga, mirjalili2014gwo}. Algorithms such as Genetic Algorithms (GA) \cite{holland1992ga}, Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) \cite{kennedy1995pso}, Differential Evolution (DE) \cite{storn1997de}, and Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO) \cite{faris2018gwo_review} balance exploration and exploitation differently. However, published benchmark literature frequently compares algorithms using unequal generation or iteration counts \cite{dhingra2025solar, rajwar2023exhaustive_review}. To address this gap, our benchmark enforces an identical budget cap of $N_{\text{eval}} = 1,500$ evaluations per seed across all five evaluated optimizer families.

\subsection{Validation Protocols \& The Temporal Data Leakage Hypothesis}\label{sec:rel_leakage}

A second core hypothesis is the \textit{Temporal Data Leakage Hypothesis}: randomized $K$-fold cross-validation on intraday time series introduces look-ahead data leakage, producing artificially inflated validation metrics.

In time series modeling, shuffling observations breaks chronological continuity, allowing future price dynamics to contaminate training sets \cite{souza2026ijcnn, makridakis2018statistical}. Despite this established risk, financial ML studies continue to employ randomized data partitioning. To eliminate look-ahead leakage, our protocol enforces a strict 60/20/20 sequential chronological split without shuffling.

\subsection{Evaluation Objectives \& The Classification-Economic Disconnect Hypothesis}\label{sec:rel_disconnect}

A third hypothesis is the \textit{Classification-Economic Disconnect Hypothesis}: tuning models solely on raw classification accuracy fails to ensure out-of-sample trading profitability under transaction costs.

Standard classification metrics such as accuracy and cross-entropy loss treat false positive and false negative errors symmetrically and fail under class imbalance \cite{chicco2020mcc}. In trading applications, uncalibrated directional signals incur substantial brokerage and slippage costs \cite{henrique2019stock_review}. To bridge this gap, we formulate a compound MCC-driven objective ($0.60\text{MCC} + 0.40F_1$) and evaluate out-of-sample performance via financial backtesting across multiple transaction fee tiers.
