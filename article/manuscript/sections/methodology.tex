\section{Materials and Methods}\label{sec:materials_methods}

Figure~\ref{fig:workflow} presents an overview of the computational workflow pipeline adopted in this study. The methodology spans six sequential stages: (i)~acquisition of high-frequency 5-minute B3 Mini-Index futures data from BovDB, (ii)~construction and min-max normalization of technical analysis indicators, (iii)~Information Gain feature selection to extract a compact 7-indicator subset, (iv)~formulation of multi-model hyperparameter search spaces across four classifier families (MLP, SVM, RF, 1D-CNN), (v)~equal-budget metaheuristic optimization ($N_{\text{eval}} = 1{,}500$) under a leakage-free 60/20/20 sequential temporal split, and (vi)~out-of-sample statistical, predictive, and financial evaluation.

\subsection{Input Dataset and Data Acquisition}\label{sec:data}

The empirical evaluation is conducted on high-frequency intraday price records of the Mini-Index futures contract (symbol: WIN), referenced to the Ibovespa index and traded on B3 (Brasil Bolsa Balc\~{a}o). The data were obtained from the BovDB repository \cite{cardoso2022bovdb, souza2024dataset}, a public academic database providing clean financial market records. This database includes 5-minute intraday candle records covering consecutive bimonthly contract maturities---specifically WING24 and WINJ24 (January--February 2024), WINJ24 and WINM24 (March--April 2024), and WINM24 and WINQ24 (May--June 2024).

These contract maturities were selected based on liquidity dominance and intense trading volume. To preserve continuous microstructural price dynamics across contract rollovers without introducing artificial volatility gaps, transitions were executed based on trading volume volume dominance. The complete dataset comprises $N = 15{,}057$ clean, consecutive 5-minute intraday bars across approximately 40 business days per contract cycle.

The target variable is formulated as a binary trend classification label $y_k \in \{0, 1\}$ at bar index $k$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
    y_k = \begin{cases} 1 \quad (\text{Uptrend}) & \text{if } P_{k+1} > P_k, \\ 0 \quad (\text{Downtrend}) & \text{if } P_{k+1} \le P_k, \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $P_k$ denotes the closing price at 5-minute bar $k$. Across the 15,057 total instances, the target distribution comprises approximately 49.2\% uptrend ($y=1$) and 50.8\% downtrend ($y=0$) instances.

\subsection{Generation and Normalization of Technical Indicators}\label{sec:indicators}

To capture short-term momentum, volatility regimes, and trend direction, technical analysis indicators were computed from the raw 5-minute intraday series. Key moving average metrics---Simple Moving Average (SMA), Exponential Moving Average (EMA), and Standard Deviation envelopes---were calculated over short lookback windows (3, 5, 7, and 9 periods) to maximize sensitivity to rapid price shifts occurring within minutes \cite{hunter1986exponentially}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sma}
    \text{SMA}_n(k) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} P_{k-i},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ema}
    \text{EMA}_n(k) = \alpha \cdot P_k + (1 - \alpha) \cdot \text{EMA}_n(k-1), \quad \alpha = \frac{2}{n+1}.
\end{equation}

To capture changes in trend slope and momentum acceleration, differential features derived from the differences between moving averages were constructed. For instance, the difference between the 7-period SMA and 3-period SMA is defined as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diff}
    \text{SMA}_{7-3}(k) = \text{SMA}_7(k) - \text{SMA}_3(k).
\end{equation}

A central preprocessing step consisted of normalizing technical indicators alongside open, high, low, and closing prices to place all features on a uniform numerical scale \cite{singh2022feature}. For each 5-minute intraday window, min-max intraday scaling was applied:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minmax}
    \text{Feature}_{\text{norm}} = \frac{\text{Feature} - \text{Low}}{\text{High} - \text{Low}},
\end{equation}
where $\text{High}$ and $\text{Low}$ correspond to the maximum and minimum price bounds observed within the same 5-minute interval. This transformation maps features into $[0, 1]$ while preserving relative position within the intraday bar. Combined with historical volume and trade frequency metrics, a total of 66 raw technical features were constructed.

\subsection{Feature Selection via Information Gain}\label{sec:feature_selection}

To address redundancy and noise inherent in 66 high-dimensional technical features \cite{souza2025iccsa, lei2012feature}, an Information Gain feature selection filter was applied:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:infogain}
    I(Y; X_j) = H(Y) - H(Y \mid X_j) = \sum_{y \in Y} \sum_{x \in X_j} p(x, y) \log_2 \left( \frac{p(x, y)}{p(x) p(y)} \right).
\end{equation}

Information Gain measures the reduction in entropy $H(Y)$ of the target trend class given technical feature $X_j$. Ranking features by Information Gain identified a compact, highly informative subset of $d=7$ indicators:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Exponential Moving Average Difference 5--3 ($\text{EMA}_{5-3}$)
    \item Exponential Moving Average Difference 7--3 ($\text{EMA}_{7-3}$)
    \item Normalized EMA Difference 5--3 ($\text{EMA}_{5-3,\text{norm}}$)
    \item Normalized EMA Difference 7--3 ($\text{EMA}_{7-3,\text{norm}}$)
    \item Normalized 9-period Simple Moving Average ($\text{SMA}_{9,\text{norm}}$)
    \item Exponential Moving Average Difference 9--3 ($\text{EMA}_{9-3}$)
    \item Normalized 7-period Exponential Moving Average ($\text{EMA}_{7,\text{norm}}$)
\end{enumerate}

Restricting input space to these 7 key indicators significantly reduces search variance and accelerates hyperparameter optimization \cite{kumar2014feature}.

\subsection{Machine Learning Model Training and Evaluation}\label{sec:training}

To prevent temporal look-ahead leakage, we enforce a strict 60/20/20 sequential chronological split protocol with no shuffling (\texttt{shuffle = false}). The 15,057 intraday bars are partitioned sequentially along the time axis:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Training Set ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}$)}: The first 60\% of chronological bars ($N_{\text{train}} = 9{,}034$ instances) for optimizing classifier connection weights and parameters.
    \item \textbf{Validation Set ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}$)}: The subsequent 20\% of chronological bars ($N_{\text{val}} = 3{,}011$ instances) for candidate fitness evaluation $f(\bm{\theta})$ during hyperparameter search.
    \item \textbf{Out-of-Sample Test Set ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{test}}$)}: The final 20\% of chronological bars ($N_{\text{test}} = 3{,}012$ instances) strictly reserved for out-of-sample predictive and financial evaluation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Metaheuristic Optimization Operators}\label{sec:optimization}

To eliminate budget inequality bias \cite{dhingra2025solar}, our benchmark protocol enforces a strict candidate evaluation cap of $N_{\text{eval}} = 1{,}500$ evaluations per random seed across all five optimizers:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
    \sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{max}}} P_{\text{size}} = N_{\text{eval}} = 1{,}500.
\end{equation}

The five evaluated optimizer families implement distinct search mechanics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Random Search (RS)}: Uniform stochastic sampling over bounds $\bm{\theta}_i = \bm{L} + \bm{r} \odot (\bm{U} - \bm{L}), \bm{r} \sim U(0,1)^D$.
    \item \textbf{Genetic Algorithm (GA)}: Tournament selection ($k=3$), uniform crossover ($P_c = 0.80$), Gaussian mutation ($P_m = 0.20$).
    \item \textbf{Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)}: Cognitive parameter $c_1 = 1.50$, social parameter $c_2 = 1.50$, inertia weight $w = 0.70$.
    \item \textbf{Differential Evolution (DE)}: \texttt{best/1/bin} strategy with mutation factor $F = 0.80$ and crossover rate $CR = 0.90$.
    \item \textbf{Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO)}: Leadership hierarchy ($\bm{\alpha}, \bm{\beta}, \bm{\delta}$) with linearly decreasing coefficient $a: 2 \to 0$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Metrics for Performance Evaluation}\label{sec:metrics}

Candidate fitness $f(\bm{\theta})$ is evaluated on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}$ using a compound objective combining Matthews Correlation Coefficient ($\operatorname{MCC}$) and $F_1$-score:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fitness}
    f(\bm{\theta}) = 0.60 \times \operatorname{MCC}(\bm{\theta}) + 0.40 \times F_1(\bm{\theta}).
\end{equation}
