\subsection{Multilayer Perceptron Search Space Formulation}\label{sec:search_space}

To evaluate hyperparameter optimization algorithms under controlled conditions, we restrict the target neural model to a standardized Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) classifier. The MLP processes the $d=7$ Information Gain feature vectors extracted from intraday 5-minute B3 Mini-Index futures data to predict directional trend labels $y \in \{0, 1\}$. 

The architectural topology, learning rates, and regularization parameters define a 6-dimensional mixed discrete-continuous search space $\boldsymbol{\Theta} \subset \mathbb{R}^6$. A candidate solution is represented as a vector $\boldsymbol{\theta} = [N_{L1}, N_{L2}, \eta, \alpha, p_{\text{drop}}, B]$, where:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $N_{L1} \in [16, 256] \subset \mathbb{Z}^+$ denotes the number of hidden units in the first hidden layer.
    \item $N_{L2} \in [0, 128] \subset \mathbb{Z}^+$ denotes the number of hidden units in the second hidden layer, where $N_{L2}=0$ corresponds to a single-hidden-layer architecture.
    \item $\eta \in [10^{-4}, 10^{-1}] \subset \mathbb{R}^+$ represents the learning rate, sampled on a logarithmic ($\log_{10}$) scale.
    \item $\alpha \in [10^{-6}, 10^{-2}] \subset \mathbb{R}^+$ represents the $L_2$ weight regularization penalty coefficient, sampled on a logarithmic scale.
    \item $p_{\text{drop}} \in [0.0, 0.50] \subset \mathbb{R}$ represents the dropout probability applied to hidden layer activations.
    \item $B \in \{32, 64, 128, 256\}$ represents the mini-batch size used during stochastic gradient updates.
\end{enumerate}

All candidate models are trained using the RMSprop optimization algorithm with binary cross-entropy loss over a maximum of 50 epochs, incorporating early stopping with a patience parameter of 3 validation epochs to mitigate overfitting.

\subsection{Compound MCC-Driven Fitness Formulation}\label{sec:fitness}

To reduce distortion from minor class imbalances and penalize false positive trading signals, optimization algorithms evaluate candidate hyperparameter vectors $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ using a compound objective function $f(\boldsymbol{\theta})$. Raw accuracy and cross-entropy loss are known to yield misleading evaluations in financial time series due to minor class skewness \cite{chicco2020mcc}. 

We define the compound fitness function $f: \boldsymbol{\Theta} \to [-0.60, 1.00]$ as a weighted combination of the Matthews Correlation Coefficient ($\text{MCC}$) and the macro-averaged $F_1$-score evaluated on out-of-sample validation data $\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fitness}
    f(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = 0.60 \times \text{MCC}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) + 0.40 \times F_1(\boldsymbol{\theta})
\end{equation}

The Matthews Correlation Coefficient is expressed as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mcc}
    \text{MCC} = \frac{TP \times TN - FP \times FN}{\sqrt{(TP + FP)(TP + FN)(TN + FP)(TN + FN)}}
\end{equation}
where $TP$, $TN$, $FP$, and $FN$ represent true positives, true negatives, false positives, and false negatives, respectively. By assigning a 60\% weight to MCC, the objective prioritizes hyperparameter configurations that achieve balanced true positive and true negative detection rates across volatile market regimes \cite{chicco2021matthews}.

\subsection{Equal Evaluation Budget Protocol}\label{sec:budget}

To control for evaluation budget disparities—a recognized limitation in published metaheuristic studies \cite{rajwar2023exhaustive_review}—all five optimization algorithms operate under a strict, identical budget cap of $N_{\text{eval}} = 1,500$ fitness evaluations per stochastic seed. 

Rather than comparing algorithms based on arbitrary generation or iteration counts, the unit of computational effort is fixed across all population-based paradigms:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
    N_{\text{eval}} = N_{\text{pop}} \times T = 1,500
\end{equation}
where $N_{\text{pop}} = 15$ candidate individuals/particles/wolves and $T = 100$ evolutionary generations or swarm iterations. Random Search draws exactly $N_{\text{eval}} = 1,500$ independent uniform samples from $\boldsymbol{\Theta}$. Each optimizer is executed across 20 independent random seeds ($S = 1, \dots, 20$) to ensure statistical confidence.

\subsection{Mathematical Formulations of Evaluated Optimizers}\label{sec:optimizers}

We benchmark five representative optimization paradigms spanning stochastic sampling, evolutionary computation, and swarm intelligence:

\subsubsection{Random Search (RS)}
Random Search serves as the unguided stochastic baseline \cite{bergstra2012random_search}. At evaluation step $t \in \{1, \dots, N_{\text{eval}}\}$, a candidate hyperparameter vector $\boldsymbol{\theta}_t$ is sampled independently from a uniform distribution over the bounded hypercube:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rs}
    \boldsymbol{\theta}_t \sim \mathcal{U}(\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{\text{min}}, \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{\text{max}})
\end{equation}
The optimal vector $\boldsymbol{\theta}^*_{\text{RS}}$ is retained as $\arg\max_{t} f(\boldsymbol{\theta}_t)$.

\subsubsection{Genetic Algorithm (GA)}
The Genetic Algorithm simulates Darwinian natural selection \cite{goldberg1989ga}. A population of $N_{\text{pop}} = 15$ binary/real-encoded individuals evolves across $T = 100$ generations. At generation $t$, parent selection is performed using tournament selection with size $k=3$. Uniform crossover is applied with probability $P_c = 0.80$, blending parent hyperparameter genes:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ga_cross}
    \boldsymbol{x}_i^{t+1} = \gamma \boldsymbol{x}_{\text{parent1}}^t + (1-\gamma) \boldsymbol{x}_{\text{parent2}}^t, \quad \gamma \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)
\end{equation}
Gaussian mutation is applied to offspring genes with probability $P_m = 0.20$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ga_mut}
    \boldsymbol{x}_{i, j}^{t+1} = \boldsymbol{x}_{i, j}^{t+1} + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)
\end{equation}
where $\sigma = 0.10 \times (\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{\text{max}, j} - \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{\text{min}, j})$. Elitism preserves the top single individual into generation $t+1$.

\subsubsection{Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)}
PSO models social foraging dynamics within a swarm of $N_{\text{pop}} = 15$ particles \cite{kennedy1995pso}. Each particle $i$ maintains a position vector $\boldsymbol{x}_i^t$ and a velocity vector $\boldsymbol{v}_i^t$. Velocity updates combine cognitive memory (personal best $\boldsymbol{p}_i$) and social memory (swarm global best $\boldsymbol{g}$):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pso_vel}
    \boldsymbol{v}_i^{t+1} = w \boldsymbol{v}_i^t + c_1 r_1 (\boldsymbol{p}_i - \boldsymbol{x}_i^t) + c_2 r_2 (\boldsymbol{g} - \boldsymbol{x}_i^t)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pso_pos}
    \boldsymbol{x}_i^{t+1} = \boldsymbol{x}_i^t + \boldsymbol{v}_i^{t+1}
\end{equation}
where inertia weight $w = 0.70$, cognitive parameter $c_1 = 1.50$, social parameter $c_2 = 1.50$, and $r_1, r_2 \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)$.

\subsubsection{Differential Evolution (DE)}
DE applies vector-differencing mutation to maintain population diversity \cite{storn1997de}. For each target vector $\boldsymbol{x}_i^t$, a mutant vector $\boldsymbol{v}_i^t$ is generated using the \texttt{best/1/bin} strategy:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:de_mut}
    \boldsymbol{v}_i^t = \boldsymbol{x}_{\text{best}}^t + F \cdot (\boldsymbol{x}_{r1}^t - \boldsymbol{x}_{r2}^t)
\end{equation}
where $r1, r2 \neq i$ are distinct random indices, and scaling factor $F = 0.80$. Binomial crossover yields trial vector $\boldsymbol{u}_i^t$ with crossover rate $CR = 0.90$. Trial vector $\boldsymbol{u}_i^t$ replaces $\boldsymbol{x}_i^t$ if $f(\boldsymbol{u}_i^t) \ge f(\boldsymbol{x}_i^t)$.

\subsubsection{Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO)}
GWO mimics the social hierarchy and hunting pack behavior of grey wolves \cite{mirjalili2014gwo}. The three fittest candidates are designated as $\alpha$ (alpha wolf), $\beta$ (beta wolf), and $\delta$ (delta wolf). Candidate wolves $\omega$ update positions relative to the leadership triad:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gwo}
    \boldsymbol{x}_i^{t+1} = \frac{\boldsymbol{x}_1 + \boldsymbol{x}_2 + \boldsymbol{x}_3}{3}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{x}_1 = \boldsymbol{x}_\alpha - \boldsymbol{A}_1 \cdot |\boldsymbol{C}_1 \cdot \boldsymbol{x}_\alpha - \boldsymbol{x}_i^t|$, with coefficient vectors $\boldsymbol{A} = 2\boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{r}_1 - \boldsymbol{a}$ linearly decaying from 2 to 0 over iterations $t=1 \dots T$.

\subsection{Statistical Hypothesis Testing Protocol}\label{sec:stats_protocol}

To evaluate out-of-sample classification disparities across the 20 stochastic seeds, non-parametric statistical hypothesis tests are conducted \cite{demsar2006statistical}:
1. **Friedman Omnibus Test**: Applied at $\alpha = 0.05$ level to test the null hypothesis that all five optimizers exhibit identical performance distributions.
2. **Post-Hoc Pairwise Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test**: Conducted for all pairwise optimizer combinations. To control the Family-Wise Error Rate (FWER), $p$-values are adjusted using the Holm-Bonferroni correction procedure.
3. **Cohen's $d$ Effect Size**: Measured to quantify the practical magnitude of performance gains:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cohen_d}
    d = \frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{s_{\text{pooled}}}, \quad s_{\text{pooled}} = \sqrt{\frac{(n_1-1)s_1^2 + (n_2-1)s_2^2}{n_1 + n_2 - 2}}
\end{equation}
Effect sizes are classified as small ($|d| \ge 0.20$), medium ($|d| \ge 0.50$), and large ($|d| \ge 0.80$).

\subsection{Financial Backtest Simulator \& Cost Sensitivity}\label{sec:backtest_protocol}

To evaluate economic utility under real-world market constraints, out-of-sample test predictions are fed into a vectorised intraday trading simulator \cite{henrique2019stock_review}. At each 5-minute bar $k$, a trading signal $S_k \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$ is generated from predicted probabilities $\hat{p}_k$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:signal}
    S_k = \begin{cases} +1 \quad (\text{Long}) & \text{if } \hat{p}_k \ge 0.50 \\ -1 \quad (\text{Short}) & \text{if } \hat{p}_k < 0.50 \end{cases}
\end{equation}

Net period return $R_{\text{net}}$ accounts for brokerage fees and exchange slippage costs $c_{\text{fee}}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:backtest}
    R_{\text{net}, k} = S_k \cdot r_k - |S_k - S_{k-1}| \cdot c_{\text{fee}}
\end{equation}
where $r_k = (P_k - P_{k-1})/P_{k-1}$ is the raw 5-minute price return. Three transaction fee scenarios are simulated: zero fees ($c_{\text{fee}} = 0.00\%$), standard exchange fees ($c_{\text{fee}} = 0.01\%$), and high-friction slippage ($c_{\text{fee}} = 0.02\%$). Performance is evaluated via Net Return \%, Annualized Sharpe Ratio, Maximum Drawdown (MDD %), and Profit Factor.
