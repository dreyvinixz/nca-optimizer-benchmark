\section{Systematic Review of Literature}\label{sec:literature_review}

Predicting short-term directional price movements in high-frequency financial markets remains one of the most demanding applications of neural computing due to low signal-to-noise ratios, rapid regime shifts, and severe non-stationarity \cite{heaton2017deep, jesus2025tda}. Supervised machine learning algorithms and technical indicator representations have been widely deployed to capture non-linear intraday patterns; however, predictive efficacy depends heavily on input feature selection, hyperparameter tuning, and validation protocol integrity \cite{singh2022feature, henrique2019stock_review}. This section presents a structured Systematic Review of Literature (SRL) examining prior work across input representations, metaheuristic optimization paradigms, and validation designs, synthesizing current state-of-the-art contributions and formalizing the critical methodological gaps that motivate our benchmark framework.

\subsection{Literature Selection Protocol and Research Questions}\label{subsec:selection_protocol}

To ensure methodological rigor and eliminate reporting bias, relevant literature was retrieved following an explicit PRISMA-guided protocol across major scholarly databases: IEEE Xplore, Scopus, Web of Science, and SpringerLink (specifically targeting \textit{Neural Computing and Applications}). The search strategy combined boolean queries for optimization paradigms (\texttt{"metaheuristic"}, \texttt{"genetic algorithm"}, \texttt{"particle swarm"}, \texttt{"differential evolution"}, \texttt{"grey wolf optimization"}), machine learning models (\texttt{"multilayer perceptron"}, \texttt{"random forest"}, \texttt{"support vector machine"}, \texttt{"1D-CNN"}), and financial application domains (\texttt{"futures market"}, \texttt{"intraday forecasting"}, \texttt{"technical indicators"}).

The review was bounded to peer-reviewed journal articles and top-tier conference proceedings published between 2015 and 2026. Inclusion criteria mandated that candidate studies: (i) evaluate automated feature selection or hyperparameter tuning on financial or complex time-series classification/forecasting, (ii) specify validation protocols and search spaces, and (iii) report empirical predictive or financial metrics. Studies lacking reproducible experimental parameters or failing to disclose optimization constraints were excluded. The systematic review is organized around three central Research Questions (RQs):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Budget Parity)}: How do stochastic metaheuristic search operators (RS, GA, PSO, DE, GWO) compare across distinct classifier backbones (RF, SVM, MLP, 1D-CNN) when restricted to a strictly equal candidate evaluation budget cap ($N_{\mathrm{eval}} = 1{,}000$)?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Temporal Leakage)}: What is the quantitative impact on out-of-sample generalization when comparing chronological sequential temporal splits against randomized cross-validation on high-frequency financial time series?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Economic Alignment)}: How effectively do classification-based validation fitness objectives (e.g., compound MCC/$F_1$ vs. weighted accuracy) align with out-of-sample intraday trading metrics (Net Return \%, Sharpe ratio, Maximum Drawdown) under realistic exchange transaction costs?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Classifier Backbones and Input Representations}\label{subsec:backbones_features}

Supervised intraday trend prediction relies heavily on the quality and information density of input features \cite{singh2022feature}. Raw high-frequency bar data (OHLCV) exhibit extreme microstructural noise and high variance. Consequently, domain-specific technical analysis indicators---spanning momentum indicators, moving averages, and volatility envelopes---are widely computed to extract directional signals \cite{billah2024nca_moving_average, hunter1986exponentially, bollinger1992using}. However, calculating dozens of technical indicators across multiple lookback windows introduces severe multicollinearity and high dimensionality, which can destabilize model training and induce overfitting \cite{lei2012feature, kumar2014feature}.

Information-theoretic feature selection provides a principled approach to reduce feature space dimensionality. Information Gain (IG) attribute filtering measures the mutual information between individual indicators and the target trend label, ranking features according to their predictive entropy reduction \cite{lei2012feature, souza2025iccsa}. Filtering out redundant or noisy indicators isolates a compact, highly informative feature core, which stabilizes fitted classification boundaries and lowers computational overhead during downstream hyperparameter search \cite{kumar2014feature}.

Downstream predictive backbones span diverse algorithmic hypothesis spaces:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Random Forest (RF)}: An ensemble of decision trees constructed via bootstrap aggregation (bagging) and random feature subset selection, highly resilient to feature noise and non-linear interactions \cite{ballings2015evaluating}.
    \item \textbf{Support Vector Machines (SVM)}: Convex maximum-margin classifiers that map non-linearly separable indicator subsets into high-dimensional feature spaces using kernel functions (e.g., Radial Basis Function) \cite{naik2019optimal}.
    \item \textbf{Multilayer Perceptron (MLP)}: Fully-connected feedforward neural networks that learn complex non-linear decision maps via error backpropagation, sensitive to layer widths, learning rates, and regularization settings \cite{ecer2020mlp_ga_pso}.
    \item \textbf{1D Convolutional Neural Network (1D-CNN)}: Deep learning architectures employing 1D temporal convolution filters and pooling layers to extract local spatial-temporal patterns directly from sequential indicator windows \cite{bandgar2025finchronos, dhingra2025solar}.
\end{itemize}
Prior research confirms that classifier ranking is strongly dependent on input feature representation, demonstrating that feature subset selection and model hyperparameter configuration must be evaluated jointly \cite{ballings2015evaluating, mutinda2026hybrid}.

\subsection{Metaheuristic Optimization Paradigms}\label{subsec:metaheuristic_paradigms}

Hyperparameter optimization is critical to prevent underfitting or severe overfitting in financial learning models. Classical gradient-based optimization methods (e.g., SGD, Adam) require smooth, differentiable loss surfaces with respect to weights, but cannot navigate discrete structural parameters (e.g., hidden neurons, filter counts, batch sizes) or non-differentiable regularization settings \cite{jovanovic2024wann, dhingra2025solar}. Population-based metaheuristic algorithms offer derivative-free global search capabilities over rugged, non-convex, mixed discrete-continuous search spaces $\bm{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ \cite{faris2018gwo_review, rajwar2023exhaustive_review}.

Four primary population-based metaheuristic families dominate the financial ML literature:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Genetic Algorithms (GA)}: Evolutionary optimization schemes simulating natural selection through population selection, uniform/single-point crossover, and mutation operators to evolve candidate solution vectors \cite{goldberg1989ga, ding2011optimizing}.
    \item \textbf{Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)}: Swarm intelligence algorithms simulating avian flocking, where individual particles dynamically update velocity vectors based on personal historical bests and global swarm best positions \cite{kennedy1995pso, gad2022pso_comprehensive}.
    \item \textbf{Differential Evolution (DE)}: Stochastic population-based vector search methods that generate trial vectors using vector differences for self-referential mutation and crossover operations \cite{storn1997de}.
    \item \textbf{Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO)}: Bio-inspired metaheuristics modeling the hunting social hierarchy ($\alpha, \beta, \delta, \omega$) and prey encirclement mechanisms of grey wolves \cite{faris2018gwo_review, al_bannoud2026accelerating}.
\end{itemize}
Random Search (RS) provides an essential baseline sampler in metaheuristic benchmarks, generating uniform random configurations across bounded parameter spaces to test whether guided search operators yield true efficiency gains beyond unguided sampling \cite{bergstra2012random_search}. 

While mathematical update formulations for these optimizers are detailed in Section~\ref{sec:methods}, existing literature frequently compares these algorithms under unconstrained or unequal candidate evaluation counts (e.g., running GA for 100 generations vs. PSO for 30 iterations without matching population sizes). Such unequal setups obscure whether reported performance gains stem from superior search operators or simply higher computational evaluation budgets \cite{dhingra2025solar, ecer2020mlp_ga_pso}.

\subsection{Critical Methodological Gaps and Research Motivation}\label{subsec:critical_gaps}

As synthesized in Table~\ref{tab:literature_gap}, three primary methodological flaws permeate the existing literature on metaheuristic financial forecasting:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{The Budget Inequality Flaw}: Prior studies routinely compare metaheuristics without enforcing a strict candidate evaluation cap ($N_{\mathrm{eval}}$). Because stochastic optimization performance scales with function evaluations, superiority claims in unconstrained studies often reflect unequal computational budgets rather than algorithmic search efficiency \cite{dhingra2025solar, ecer2020mlp_ga_pso}.
    \item \textit{The Temporal Data Leakage Flaw}: Financial machine learning research frequently relies on randomized $K$-fold cross-validation or data shuffling \cite{simatupang2026stock, makridakis2018statistical}. Shuffling sequential intraday price bars allows future market information to contaminate historical training folds, yielding artificially inflated predictive accuracy scores that collapse during live execution.
    \item \textit{The Classification-Economic Disconnect Flaw}: Hyperparameter tuning is overwhelmingly conducted against raw accuracy or cross-entropy loss \cite{chicco2020mcc, souza2025iccsa}. In high-frequency futures trading, raw accuracy ignores class imbalance and trade execution costs. A classifier achieving high nominal accuracy can incur net financial losses once exchange transaction fees and slippage are deducted.
\end{enumerate}

To address these limitations, this paper establishes an auditable multi-optimizer benchmark framework on 15,057 five-minute B3 Mini-Index futures bars. Leveraging Information Gain feature selection to extract a compact 7-indicator subset, we systematically evaluate five optimizer families (RS, GA, PSO, DE, GWO) across four diverse model backbones (MLP, SVM, RF, 1D-CNN). All optimizers operate under a strictly locked candidate evaluation cap of $N_{\mathrm{eval}} = 1{,}000$ evaluations per seed across three common random seeds within a leakage-free 60/20/20 chronological partition. Furthermore, we contrast two distinct validation fitness objectives (a compound $0.60\,\mathrm{MCC} + 0.40\,F_1$ objective vs. weighted accuracy) and evaluate held-out trading profitability under realistic exchange transaction costs.
